Intelligent Coastal and Offshore Geotechnics and Infrastructure (ICOGI)

We combine field measurements, experiments, modelling and AI to improve offshore and coastal infrastructure design.

ICOGI is a research group at RMIT University. Our current projects focus on offshore wind foundations: understanding installation effects, predicting response to repeated loading, and estimating seabed properties from site investigation data.

Cutting Offshore Wind Energy Costs through AI-Enhanced Foundation Design

Our research

ICOGI investigates ground behaviour and soil-foundation interaction for offshore and coastal infrastructure. Our current AEA Ignite work focuses on offshore wind foundations, with linked studies of installation, cyclic response and ground characterisation.

Current research

This research was supported by the Australian Government Department of Education through Australia’s Economic Accelerator (AEA) Ignite program (Grant No. IG250201301).

  • Vibratory monopile installation in sand

    We are developing a model that combines the material point method and discrete element method (MPM-DEM) to study vibratory monopile installation in sand. The aim is to predict how installation changes density, stress and pore-water pressure, and how these changes affect foundation stiffness and lateral capacity. Planned validation uses soil tests, cone penetration benchmarks and model-scale installation tests.

  • I-PISA: long-term cyclic monopile response

    We are developing Intelligent Pile-Soil Analysis (I-PISA) to predict the long-term response of monopiles in sand to repeated loading. The approach combines physics-based high-cycle accumulation modelling with machine-learning-assisted calibration using centrifuge, soil test and finite-element datasets. The aim is faster prediction of accumulated movement, rotation and stiffness changes under different drainage conditions.

  • Shear-wave velocity prediction from CPT

    We combine cone penetration test (CPT) measurements with geotechnical knowledge to predict shear-wave velocity using machine learning. Data from seven North Sea projects support model development and testing. Entire measurement locations are held out from training to assess performance at unseen locations. The aim is to complement sparse seismic measurements and improve offshore ground characterisation.

Research themes

  • Ground characterisation

    Interpreting field measurements and soil behaviour to characterise seabed and coastal ground conditions.

  • Offshore and coastal foundations

    Investigating how ground conditions, installation and repeated loading affect foundation movement, stiffness and capacity.

  • Computational geotechnics

    Using detailed simulations to understand soil-foundation interaction and build faster engineering models.

  • AI, data and monitoring

    Combining machine learning, geotechnical knowledge and measurement data to assess ground and infrastructure behaviour.

  • Research into practice

    Connecting research with site assessment and foundation design through industry collaboration and validation.

Coastal applications

We welcome coastal collaborations on ground characterisation and foundation behaviour for ports, harbours and nearshore infrastructure.

How we work

We link field evidence and physical testing with numerical modelling and data analysis. We assess predictions against available measurements, focusing on accuracy, uncertainty and the conditions in which a model can be used.
