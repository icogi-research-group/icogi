About ICOGI

Research for the next generation of offshore infrastructure

The ICOGI Research Lab investigates how coastal and offshore ground conditions, foundations and infrastructure interact across their lifecycle — from site characterisation and installation to long-term performance.

Our research connects field and site-investigation data, physical experiments, advanced numerical modelling and artificial intelligence to understand complex soil–infrastructure behaviour and translate that understanding into engineering decisions.

Rather than treating these approaches separately, we integrate physical evidence, computational mechanics and data-driven methods to develop models that are explainable, validated and useful in practice.

Offshore infrastructure operates in an environment where the ground is variable, loading is uncertain and many of the mechanisms controlling long-term performance cannot be observed directly. Our goal is not simply to develop more complex models, but to turn complex offshore behaviour into knowledge and prediction methods that engineers can understand, validate and use.

Research Areas

Five interconnected research themes

Our research capabilities are designed to work together, connecting seabed characterisation and experimental evidence with high-fidelity simulation, efficient prediction and engineering decision-making.

01
Intelligent ground characterisation and modelling
The seabed is not a single set of design parameters. ICOGI combines geotechnical measurements, geological context, engineering knowledge and data-driven methods to develop ground models that preserve this complexity while remaining useful for engineering analysis.
02
Offshore foundation systems
We investigate the mechanisms governing the behaviour of monopiles and other offshore foundation systems from installation through long-term operation, examining soil–foundation interaction, stiffness and capacity under repeated environmental loading.
03
Computational offshore geotechnics
High-fidelity numerical models allow us to investigate stresses, deformation and soil–structure interaction at scales that cannot always be measured experimentally. We use numerical simulation as a computational laboratory — to test mechanisms and generate evidence.
04
AI, data and monitoring
ICOGI investigates how site-investigation, experimental, numerical and monitoring data can complement — not replace — physical understanding. Our work includes machine learning, surrogate modelling, uncertainty-aware prediction and data-informed characterisation.
05
Research translated to practice
ICOGI works to shorten the distance between research findings and engineering use. We develop computational workflows, benchmark problems, design methods, datasets and modelling tools that can be evaluated, reproduced and progressively translated into practice.
→
One research ecosystem, multiple forms of evidence
Our research capabilities are designed to work together. Field and site evidence establish real conditions. Physical experiments provide controlled evidence. High-fidelity modelling reveals mechanisms. Efficient prediction translates analyses. Validation checks accuracy and applicability.

Research Capabilities

How our evidence chain works

Our objective is not greater computational sophistication for its own sake — it is better-supported engineering decisions.

🌊
Field and site evidence
CPT/SCPT measurements, monitoring data, geological information and other site evidence establish the real conditions our models must explain.
🔬
Physical experimentation
Laboratory and physical modelling provide controlled evidence of mechanisms, response and failure.
💻
High-fidelity numerical modelling
Advanced simulations reveal stress redistribution, deformation and soil–foundation mechanisms that are difficult to measure directly.
⚡
Efficient engineering prediction
Reduced-order models, surrogate models and data-driven methods translate detailed analyses into computationally efficient prediction.
✓
Validation and uncertainty
Predictions are tested against available evidence, with attention to accuracy, uncertainty, applicability and limitations.
🏗
Engineering interpretation
Actionable knowledge for site characterisation, foundation assessment, design optimisation, monitoring and long-term performance evaluation.

Current Projects

Active research

ICOGI research follows an evidence chain connecting offshore environment, site evidence, physical mechanisms, computational modelling and engineering decisions.

Installation effects on offshore foundations
Foundation installation changes the soil before operational loading even begins. This research investigates installation-induced changes in soil stress, density and stiffness, and examines how this altered ground state influences subsequent capacity, deformation and cyclic response. Numerical modelling is used to connect the installation process with the foundation behaviour engineers ultimately need to predict.
Long-term cyclic response of offshore monopiles
Offshore wind foundations experience millions of cycles of wind, wave and operational loading. Small changes accumulated over time can influence displacement, rotation and foundation stiffness. This research combines experimental evidence, numerical simulation and data-informed modelling to investigate cyclic soil–pile interaction and develop efficient methods for long-term foundation assessment.
Next-generation PISA-based monopile design
This research develops computationally efficient methods for predicting the lateral response of offshore monopiles while retaining the key soil–pile mechanisms represented by advanced numerical models. Building on the PISA framework, the research investigates how soil state and internal friction angle of sand can be incorporated into soil-reaction relationships and ultimate-limit-state assessment, extending design capability beyond existing site-specific formulations.
Intelligent offshore ground characterisation and prediction
Offshore site investigations generate large quantities of geotechnical data, but engineering prediction often treats measurements independently and gives limited consideration to geological context or information available from neighbouring projects. This research investigates a different approach: combining CPT/SCPT measurements, measured soil stiffness, geological descriptors, empirical relationships and machine learning to build transferable models of offshore ground behaviour. The longer-term objective is an intelligent ground-characterisation framework that identifies what is known at a site, what can defensibly be learned from comparable sites, and where prediction uncertainty remains high.

The Evidence Chain

Offshore environment → Site evidence → Physical mechanisms → Computational model → Efficient prediction → Validation & uncertainty → Engineering decision

This enables detailed scientific investigation to inform practical questions such as:

What ground conditions control foundation response?

What changes during installation?

How will response accumulate under cyclic loading?

Which model is appropriate for a particular site?

Can knowledge be transferred between offshore projects?

How reliable is a prediction outside the conditions used to develop it?
